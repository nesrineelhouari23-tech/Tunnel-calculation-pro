1. Calculation assumptions

The Peck (1969) method describes the transverse surface settlement profile induced by tunnel excavation using a symmetric Gaussian distribution centred on the tunnel axis.

The calculation is based on a homogeneous ground approximation and a greenfield condition, meaning that the influence of buildings, foundations and underground utilities is not explicitly considered.

The settlement volume per unit tunnel length is assumed to correspond to the product of the volume loss ratio and the excavated tunnel cross-sectional area. The trough-width parameter is defined by i = K × z₀, where K is entered manually according to the considered ground conditions.

2. Calculation model

The calculation follows the successive relationships below:

Excavated tunnel area:

A = πD² / 4

Settlement volume per unit tunnel length:

Vs = (VL / 100) × A

Since the calculation is performed per unit tunnel length, Vs is expressed in m².

Settlement trough-width parameter:

i = K × z₀

Maximum settlement magnitude:

Smax = Vs / (√(2π) × i)

Vertical settlement at a transverse distance x:

Sv(x) = −Smax × exp[−x² / (2i²)]

3. Sign convention

Downward vertical displacement is considered negative, whereas upward displacement is positive. Consequently, the calculated vertical settlement Sv(x) is negative. In contrast, Smax and Vs represent positive magnitudes.

The maximum settlement magnitude occurs directly above the tunnel axis (x = 0). It progressively decreases with increasing transverse distance from the axis.

4. Interpretation of results

The parameter i characterises the width of the settlement trough. A larger value of i produces a wider and, for a fixed settlement volume, generally shallower trough.

Smax represents the maximum settlement magnitude, while Sv(x) gives the signed vertical displacement at the selected transverse position. The graph and results table illustrate the spatial distribution of settlement above the tunnel.

5. Scope and limitations

This analytical approach provides an approximate estimate of the transverse ground settlement profile under greenfield conditions. It is particularly useful for preliminary assessments and parametric studies.

The simplified model does not explicitly account for complex soil layering, groundwater effects, construction sequence, three-dimensional effects or soil–structure interaction. The results should therefore be interpreted as estimates and, where possible, compared with site-specific measurements or calibrated numerical analyses.

1. Calculation assumptions

The horizontal ground displacement is estimated using the relationship proposed by O'Reilly and New (1982). This approach links the transverse horizontal displacement at the ground surface to the corresponding vertical settlement through a simplified radial displacement-vector assumption.

The calculation uses the vertical settlement profile obtained from the Gaussian model. It assumes a homogeneous ground approximation and greenfield conditions, without explicitly modelling buildings, foundations or underground utilities.

2. Calculation model

The horizontal displacement is calculated as follows:

Horizontal displacement:

uh(x) = (x / z0) × Sv(x)

The vertical settlement is described by the Gaussian distribution:

Sv(x) = −Smax × exp[−x² / (2i²)]

The trough-width parameter is:

i = K × z0

Where:

  • uh(x): horizontal ground displacement (m).
  • x: signed transverse distance from the tunnel axis (m).
  • z0: depth of the tunnel axis (m).
  • Sv(x): signed vertical surface displacement (m).
  • Smax: maximum settlement magnitude (m).
  • i: settlement trough-width parameter (m).
  • K: dimensionless trough-width coefficient.

3. Sign convention

The transverse coordinate x is defined as negative on the left side of the tunnel axis and positive on the right side. Downward vertical settlement is negative.

Consequently, the calculated horizontal displacement is positive on the left side (x < 0) and negative on the right side (x > 0). This sign convention indicates horizontal movements directed towards the tunnel axis.

At the tunnel axis (x = 0), the horizontal displacement is zero. For the Gaussian settlement profile, the maximum absolute horizontal displacement occurs at x = −i and x = +i, with opposite signs.

4. Interpretation of results

The horizontal displacement varies with the transverse position relative to the tunnel axis. Its magnitude increases from zero at the axis, reaches a maximum on either side, and then decreases with increasing distance.

The displacement profile is antisymmetric: the magnitudes on both sides are equal under the assumed symmetric Gaussian settlement profile, but their signs are opposite.

5. Scope and limitations

This relationship provides an approximate estimate of transverse horizontal surface displacement under greenfield conditions. It is useful for preliminary assessments and for studying the relationship between vertical and horizontal ground movements.

The simplified approach does not explicitly account for soil heterogeneity, groundwater conditions, construction sequence, three-dimensional effects or soil–structure interaction. Results should therefore be interpreted with regard to the assumptions of the model and compared, where possible, with field measurements or calibrated numerical analyses.
